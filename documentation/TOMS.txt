CHAPTER II


Design and Development of an RFID-Based POS and Passenger Monitoring System for Public Minibus Transportation: A Systematic Literature Review
Abstract
Public transportation, particularly minibuses, still relies on manual fare collection and paper tickets, leading to inaccurate passenger counts and revenue inconsistencies. These inefficiencies make it difficult to monitor real-time occupancy, resulting in poor capacity management and potential income loss. This study focuses on conducting a Systematic Literature Review (SLR) on microcontroller-based systems designed for accurate passenger occupancy monitoring.  Using PRISMA guidelines, 2,020 records were identified and filtered down to 22 primary studies for technical analysis. Findings show that modern systems use microcontrollers like the ESP32 combined with RFID, optical sensors, or camera-based solutions for real-time passenger detection. Notably, ESP32-CAM modules using machine learning (e.g., FOMO algorithm) achieve up to 96% accuracy, while hybrid optical-RFID systems can reach near 100% counting accuracy through backend verification. GPS modules are also integrated to associate occupancy data with routes and stops. Despite these advancements, challenges such as counting errors during crowding, hardware costs, and unreliable connectivity persist. This study addresses these gaps by proposing a cost-effective handheld and sensor-assisted system that prioritizes precise occupancy tracking, enabling better capacity management, reduced fraud, and improved decision-making in public transportation.
Keywords: RFID, Fare Collection, Passenger Counting, IoT, Android POS Terminal.
2.1 Introduction
Traditional fare collection and passenger monitoring in public transportation is a major problem worldwide, especially in developing countries like the Philippines where manual systems are still used. Using paper tickets and manual counting often leads to revenue loss and frequent arguments over incorrect change (Zaman et al., 2023; Ingabire, 2023). These old manual methods are very slow, and conductors often make mistakes during daily bus trips (Agarwal et al., 2022; Ingabire, 2023). Research indicates that transitioning to automated systems can significantly reduce these inefficiencies, potentially decreasing transit operating costs by up to 30% (Adebiyi et al., 2021). While advanced electronic systems exist in developed countries, they are usually too expensive for small transport businesses in local areas (Murdan et al., 2020).
In the Philippines, mini-buses still rely on conductors to count passengers and collect money by hand. Research shows that this is an ineffective way to record exact fares because manual collection is highly susceptible to human error, vulnerable to fraud, and lacks transparency (Kannan et al., 2020; Ingabire, 2023). This manual setup often enables conductor malpractice, such as failing to issue tickets, which directly results in inaccurate passenger manifests and significant revenue leakage (Ingabire, 2023). These systems do not give passengers digital receipts, leaving them with no proof of payment (Kulange et al., 2025). Also, many passengers struggle daily because they do not have the exact "change" needed for the bus fare (Jenita et al., 2026). As cities grow, there is a strong need for a more reliable way to track passengers and their payments to ensure profit accuracy and prevent fraud (Kulange et al., 2025; Ingabire, 2023).
New technology using microcontrollers, RFID cards, and GPS can significantly improve public transport (Nirmala et al., 2024). These systems can track vehicle locations and keep a digital record of every payment made (Rajkumar et al., 2018). Advanced implementations now incorporate machine learning techniques like FOMO (Faster Objects, More Objects) on ESP32-CAM modules to detect seat vacancy and count passengers with up to 95.3% accuracy (Gonzales et al., 2025). Furthermore, integrating dual-verification architectures, such as optical-RFID synchronization, can achieve 100% accuracy in counting events, ensuring that every passenger is accounted for and fraud is eliminated (Lam et al., 2026). Recent research also explores frictionless fare collection by matching smartphone location data with vehicle trajectories using Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) and Longest Common Subsequence (LCSS) algorithms (Ghorbankhani, 2024). Developments even utilize Random Forest algorithms to optimize profit structures and predict passenger demand based on cloud-stored data (Vadivel et al., 2024).
Using RFID cards has already worked well in metro systems to stop long lines, prevent ticketing loopholes, and make travel more secure (Suvarna et al., 2022). However, some studies show that keeping these systems secure and paying for the equipment are still big challenges (Kulange et al., 2025). Most existing systems also lack the proper implementation of reusable cards that can track travel history while reducing paper waste in an eco-friendly way (Pijdurkar et al., 2024; Ingabire, 2023).
This systematic literature review (SLR) aims to study existing RFID-based Point-of-Sale (POS) and passenger monitoring systems for mini-buses. It evaluates the hardware currently being built (Bhavani et al., 2025), how they record fares (Iswarya et al., 2022), and what is still missing for a cheap and easy-to-use system. Following PRISMA guidelines, the study identified an initial pool of 2,020 records, 2,019 from the Google Scholar database and 1 from an institutional repository (air.ashesi.edu.gh), which was systematically screened to select 22 primary studies for detailed technical analysis and synthesis. By using the CIMO framework, this review answers how a new system can fix the manual problems in Iligan City. The main goal is to see how an RFID handheld device and reusable cards can make passenger counting and fare collection more accurate than the old manual way, effectively maximizing profit accuracy and eliminating fraud (Lam et al., 2026; Ingabire, 2023).


2.2 Research Methods
This study utilized a Systematic Literature Review (SLR) methodology to identify existing RFID-based Point-of-Sale (POS) and passenger monitoring technologies for public minibus transportation. The review follows the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) reporting guidelines and is composed of five distinct steps. The first step involves framing clear and focused research questions regarding manual fare issues using collaborative writing tools,  the second step includes identifying and gathering relevant literature from an initial pool of 2,020 records, consisting of 2,019 records retrieved from the Google Scholar database using the Harzing tool and 1 record identified from an institutional repository (air.ashesi.edu.gh); the third step entails critically appraising and selecting studies through Rayyan for abstract screening and Google Scholar for full-text reviews, applying inclusion criteria such as the use of microcontroller-based logic, IoT-integrated hardware architectures, and high-quality technical gray literature (Suvarna et al., 2022; Ingabire, 2023); the fourth step summarizes and synthesizes key findings from the 22 selected primary studies using Google Spreadsheets for data extraction, focusing on accurate passenger counting, profit accuracy, and the prevention of conductor fraud (Gonzales et al., 2025; Lam et al., 2026; Vadivel et al., 2024); and finally, the fifth step evaluates the applicability of these results to real-world minibus scenarios in Iligan City.

2.2.1. Review Protocol 
This phase involved the formulation of a systematic literature review (SLR) protocol to ensure transparency, repeatability, and to reduce bias during the research process. This protocol acts as a detailed plan outlining the methodology and processes followed throughout the review, which includes the following: (i) the establishment of focused research questions using Google Docs to address the challenges of manual ticketing, real-time passenger occupancy monitoring, and revenue leakage in Iligan City; (ii) a systematic search strategy executed through Harzing’s Publish or Perish to gather an initial pool of 2,020 records, including 2,019 from Google Scholar and 1 from the air.ashesi.edu.gh repository; (iii) title, abstract, and full-text screening using Rayyan and Google Scholar to apply inclusion and exclusion criteria focused on microcontroller-based logic, automated counting sensors, and RFID-integrated systems (Suvarna et al., 2022; Ingabire, 2023); (iv) quality assessment and data extraction via Google Spreadsheets to synthesize findings regarding how RFID, GPS, and occupancy detection algorithms automate fare recording to maximize profit accuracy and prevent conductor fraud (Gonzales et al., 2025; Lam et al., 2026); and (v) reference management utilizing Mendeley to systematically organize and format bibliographic citations throughout the review. 
2.2.2. Research Question
The primary goal of this research is to identify and assess existing microcontroller-based passenger monitoring and fare collection technologies used in the public transportation sector. This includes identifying the specific hardware configurations, such as RFID readers, GPS modules, and handheld Point-of-Sale (POS) devices, as well as the software logic used for fare calculation. The review also aims to uncover existing challenges and gaps in the literature to guide the development of a more accurate and automated system for local minibuses. Accordingly, the study is structured around the following research questions based on the CIMO (Context, Intervention, Mechanism, Outcome) framework:
RQ1: What are the challenges and limitations of the current manual passenger counting and fare monitoring systems in public utility vehicles, particularly in local settings like Iligan City?
RQ2 : How can an RFID-based Point-of-Sale (POS)and passenger monitoring system be designed using RFID cards and handheld devices to effectively record passenger destinations and fares?
RQ3 : Through what mechanisms do RFID scanning and seat card systems automate the process of passenger counting and fare recording in public transportation? 
RQ4 : How effective is an automated RFID-based system in improving the accuracy of passenger monitoring and fare collection compared to traditional manual methods? 


2.2.3. Search Strategy
This phase involves the identification of relevant works through the Harzing search engine, focusing on the Google Scholar database to ensure a wide-reaching search of both academic journals and technical conference papers. The search was conducted on March 11, 12, and 13, 2026, to gather an initial pool of 2,020 records. This total consists of 2,019 records retrieved via Harzing’s Publish or Perish and 1 record identified from an institutional repository (air.ashesi.edu.gh). This approach was chosen to capture the most recent developments in microcontroller-based transit systems found in specialized engineering journals and high-quality technical gray literature. The search queries were designed to cover the hardware, payment logic, and occupancy monitoring aspects of the study. Studies focusing on personal vehicles or unrelated industries like inventory and logistics (EC3, EC4) were excluded to maintain the focus on public transport. By focusing on technical implementations and system accuracy (IC5), the review may have limited coverage of broader transport policies or government regulations.

2.2.5. Quality Assessment
This process involved the formal evaluation of the 22 selected publications using a standardized quality checklist to ensure the technical reliability of the research. Each study was appraised based on four core criteria (Q1–Q4) focusing on the clarity of objectives, the technical detail of the hardware and software for reproducibility, the validity of testing through simulations or prototypes, and the strength of the conclusions. To quantify the quality, each criterion was scored on a 1–4 scale, where a score of 4 represents a "Fully Met" requirement with detailed evidence, and 1 represents a "Not Met" status. Studies were then categorized into High, Medium, or Low quality based on their total points. This rigorous filtering ensures that only studies with reliable data on RFID and POS systems, such as those providing specific circuit logic or transaction metrics, are used to inform the development of the system for Iligan City.

	2.2.8 Limitations of the Review 


This systematic review is not without its limitations and potential risks of bias. Firstly, the study does not apply meta-analyses, focusing instead on a qualitative and technical synthesis of hardware logic and system implementations for RFID-based POS terminals and passenger monitoring. While a comprehensive search strategy was executed using the Harzing tool to gather an initial pool of 2,020 records from Google Scholar and the Ashesi University repository, there is a possibility that relevant publications were missed because the search was restricted to specific digital databases. Consequently, this review does not provide an exhaustive list of all global microcontroller-based transport monitoring technologies, as other engineering-specific libraries might contain additional literature. Furthermore, no formal snowballing was performed during the selection process, meaning that the references of the 22 primary studies were not independently searched to identify further relevant data.
Other inherent biases include a language bias, as the review only included English-language publications, which may have excluded region-specific innovations published in other languages. A recency bias is also present due to the focus on the 2018–2026 timeframe; while this ensured the inclusion of modern hardware like the ESP32 and advanced machine learning models (Gonzales et al., 2025), it may have overlooked older foundational studies in electronic fare collection. Finally, while the review prioritized peer-reviewed articles and conference papers to ensure technical reliability, it made a deliberate effort to include high-quality technical gray literature from institutional repositories (e.g., Ingabire, 2023) to capture practical insights into the unique operations of public utility vehicles. Despite these efforts, some unpublished industry prototypes or proprietary local systems might remain unexamined.

2.3.2. Emerging Trends and Advancements
Several emerging trends and advancements in microcontroller-based transport monitoring and fare collection have been identified from the literature (S1–S22). These developments focus on maximizing operational efficiency, revenue transparency, and commuter convenience by transitioning from manual paper-based systems to integrated IoT ecosystems.
Real-time IoT and Cloud Integration
Modern solutions have moved beyond simple data logging to active profit optimization. Utilizing microcontrollers like the ESP32 and NodeMCU, current systems send real-time transaction data to cloud platforms for immediate oversight. This allows transport owners to monitor passenger counts and revenue streams instantly via mobile dashboards, significantly reducing the risk of lost income (Kulange et al., 2025; Jenita et al., 2026; Vadivel et al., 2024).
Automated Passenger Counting (APC) and AI
A major trend is the elimination of manual counting through high-precision sensor integration. While early systems relied on Infrared (IR) sensors and force-sensitive resistors (FSRs), newer research utilizes the FOMO algorithm on ESP32-CAM (Gonzales et al., 2025) and dual-verification architectures to achieve 100% counting accuracy (Lam et al., 2026). This ensures that vehicle occupancy perfectly matches the number of fares collected without human intervention (Murdan et al., 2020; Haque et al., 2024).
GPS-Based Distance Calculation and Frictionless Fare
To ensure fare fairness, systems are shifting toward automated distance-based billing. Many designs now include GPS modules (NEO-6M) to track where a passenger boards and alights, automatically deducting the correct amount from an RFID card (Zaman et al., 2023; Suthagar, 2023). Emerging "frictionless" methods even use smartphone trajectory matching to automate this process without requiring a physical "tap" from the commuter.
Portable Handheld POS and Mobile Accessibility
Recent research has shifted focus from bulky, fixed hardware to compact, handheld POS devices and integrated web portals. These platforms empower passengers to recharge cards remotely, check real-time balances, and track bus locations, making the system highly accessible and convenient (Pijdurkar et al., 2024; Jenita et al., 2026).
Sustainable and Reusable RFID Systems
There is a growing emphasis on "green" transport by replacing single-use paper tickets with reusable RFID cards. Evidence shows that this transition reduces environmental waste and operational costs while providing a more durable solution for the high-frequency nature of public utility vehicles (Agarwal et al., 2022).
Offline Data Storage and Reliability
Addressing the challenges of unstable mobile networks, modern implementations prioritize offline-first logic. Local storage and database synchronization allow devices to log transactions in signal "dead zones," ensuring that data is preserved and synced with the cloud once connectivity is restored. This reliability is critical for preventing conductor malpractice and ensuring total revenue transparency (Ingabire, 2023; Iswarya et al., 2022).
2.3.3. Current Challenges and Research Gaps
Based on the 22 reviewed papers, several challenges and research gaps in applying microcontroller-based technologies to public minibus transportation have been revealed. These challenges are multifaceted, spanning from technical hardware limitations and connectivity issues to the unique operational needs of local transport.


Current Challenges
Several current challenges and limitations in microcontroller-based transport monitoring have been identified from the literature (S1–S22). These issues highlight the critical gap between theoretical designs and the practical, high-demand operational environment of public utility vehicles (PUVs) in regions like Iligan City.
Inaccurate Real-Time Passenger Manifests: Traditional manual operations are highly susceptible to revenue leakage and human logging errors. Even within automated systems, researchers note that basic Infrared (IR) and ultrasonic sensors often struggle with precision when passengers move in close proximity at the doorway, making it difficult to maintain a flawless real-time count without advanced sensor synchronization or machine learning verification (Haque et al., 2024; Murdan et al., 2020).
Connectivity and Synchronization Challenges: Maintaining a stable cloud connection in a moving transit vehicle remains a primary engineering obstacle. Most standard IoT architectures fail to synchronize data effectively when a vehicle enters a cellular signal "dead zone." This operational reality necessitates the implementation of robust offline-to-online relational logic to ensure no fare or tracking data is permanently lost during transit (Kulange et al., 2025; Nirmala et al., 2024).
Portability and Hardware Robustness: Many existing RFID-based designs are structurally intended for large, stationary bus terminals or train station gates rather than compact, mobile units. Public utility minibuses require dense, battery-powered handheld devices capable of withstanding constant vehicle vibration, high thermal heat, and rigorous physical handling by conductors (Bhavani et al., 2025; Kalpana et al., 2022).
High Initial Costs and Resistance to Change: Implementing smart e-ticketing ecosystems requires a notable initial investment in physical hardware and backend infrastructure. Research indicates that small-scale transport operators and local cooperatives are often resistant to these upfront costs, preferring traditional manual collection despite its documented financial inefficiency (Murdan et al., 2020; Zaman et al., 2023).
Data Security and Fare Transparency: The systemic shift to automated billing raises valid security concerns regarding RFID card cloning, unauthorized database access, and fraudulent transactions. Establishing a secure, tamper-proof logging system using unique hardware IDs or encrypted data transmission protocols is essential for building trust between transport operators and commuters (Iswarya et al., 2022; Kulange et al., 2025).


Research Gaps
Analysis of the existing literature (S1–S22) reveals several critical research gaps regarding the intersection of hardware portability and localized operational logic, which the design and development of the proposed system aims to address. A primary gap exists in the development of a unified handheld POS device that integrates automatic passenger counting; while high-precision monitoring has been achieved through machine learning (Gonzales et al., 2025) and dual-verification architectures (Lam et al., 2026), these features are typically confined to fixed gate infrastructure rather than portable units suitable for mobile conductors. Furthermore, a significant discrepancy is found in the card distribution model, where most studies assume personal "smart card" ownership (Agarwal et al., 2022; Pijdurkar et al., 2024), leaving a void in the literature regarding reusable, seat-based RFID markers which are more practical for high-turnover public utility vehicles (PUVs). This lack of localized adaptation extends to technical reliability, as current frameworks often overlook energy-efficient, intermittent synchronization tailored for rugged transit environments (Ingabire, 2023; Kulange et al., 2025). Ultimately, most transport research prioritizes standardized metro systems (Zaman et al., 2023), resulting in a scarcity of low-cost, open-source solutions designed for the unique, unscheduled, and "konduktor-led" operations characteristic of the transport sector. The proposed system addresses these technical limitations by tailoring these global methodologies directly to the unique public utility vehicle constraints within Iligan City. 


2.3.4. Derived Conceptual Framework
The synthesis of the 22 reviewed studies (S1–S22) provided a comprehensive foundation for the Design and Development of an RFID-Based POS and Passenger Monitoring System, identifying the technical requirements and operational constraints necessary for successful implementation.
Firstly, the review identified essential hardware platforms, noting a definitive shift from basic Arduino prototypes (Rajkumar, 2020; Zaman et al., 2023) toward IoT-enabled microcontrollers like the ESP32 for dual-core processing and mobile connectivity (Kulange et al., 2025; Nirmala et al., 2024). These platforms, paired with MFRC522 RFID modules and GPS Neo-6M sensors (Kannan et al., 2020), establish the baseline for real-time passenger identification and distance-based fare monitoring.
Secondly, emerging trends highlighted a transition toward intelligent automation, including the use of Machine Learning (FOMO algorithm) on ESP32-CAM for high-precision seat occupancy detection (Gonzales et al., 2025) and dual-verification architectures to achieve 100% counting accuracy (Lam et al., 2026). Furthermore, the integration of cloud-based algorithms, such as Random Forest, was identified as a key method for optimizing profit and predicting passenger demand (Vadivel et al., 2024; Jenita et al., 2026).
Thirdly, critical research gaps were identified, such as the persistent challenge of cloud synchronization in signal "dead zones" (Kulange et al., 2025; Ingabire, 2023) and a lack of unified handheld devices that combine fare collection with automated counting. A significant void was also noted regarding reusable, seat-based RFID card logic, as most literature assumes personal card ownership rather than vehicle-specific markers suitable for high-turnover public utility vehicles.
Drawing from these insights, the conceptual framework was derived to bridge the gap between high-tech IoT trends and the rugged operational realities of Iligan City. The framework outlines that while existing MCUs provide the baseline, the unique challenges of connectivity loss and the "konduktor-led" system necessitate a specialized approach. The proposed system integrates optical-RFID synchronization, offline-ready database logic, and a reusable seat-marker system within a single handheld terminal to ensure revenue transparency and automated manifest generation for the local minibus environment.
2.3.5. Synthesis of Research Findings
Answers to the Research Questions:
RQ1: Challenges of Manual Systems Traditional fare collection methods are universally defined by chronic revenue leakage, human logging errors, and an absolute lack of operational transparency. Manual ticketing is inherently slow, serves as a frequent catalyst for commuter-conductor disputes, and generates significant environmental waste through single-use paper receipts (Murdan et al., 2020; Zaman et al., 2023).
RQ2: Design of RFID-Based POS High-performing system architectures consistently prioritize the ESP32 microcontroller for its dual-core processing and native IoT capabilities, seamlessly integrating the MFRC522 RFID module for passenger identification and the NEO-6M/7M GPS module for distance tracking. A critical, non-negotiable architectural requirement identified across the literature is offline-first local storage logic to maintain transaction data integrity when transit vehicles pass through cellular signal "dead zones" (Ingabire, 2023; Kulange et al., 2025).
RQ3: Automation and Counting Mechanisms System automation relies heavily on "Dual-Punch" GPS coordinates for dynamic, distance-based fare indexing alongside Automated Passenger Counting (APC) mechanisms. While legacy frameworks heavily relied on rudimentary hardware like infrared (IR) beam-break sensors, state-of-the-art implementations utilize the edge-AI FOMO machine learning algorithm and real-time optical-RFID synchronization to ensure physical passenger manifests consistently match digital occupancy metrics (Gonzales et al., 2025; Lam et al., 2026).
RQ4: Effectiveness vs. Traditional Methods Automated transit monitoring platforms reduce overall vehicle operational costs by approximately 30% while simultaneously achieving up to 100% passenger counting accuracy. By eliminating the "Human Error Fallacy," these unified hardware-software ecosystems systematically mitigate internal fraud, maximize daily revenue yield, and optimize the overall commuter experience through accelerated vehicle boarding times and secure digital transaction logging.


Synthesis
The synthesis of research findings from the 22 primary studies (S1–S22) identifies a significant technological evolution in public transportation monitoring and fare collection. Initially, many systems utilized the Arduino platform for early-stage prototypes focused on basic RFID and GPS interfacing. However, recent research demonstrates a definitive shift toward IoT-enabled microcontrollers like the ESP32 and ESP8266, which are now preferred for their dual-core processing and integrated wireless connectivity. This technical infrastructure supports more complex operations, such as real-time cloud synchronization and high-speed reporting, which are essential for managing modern transit environments.
Regarding fare management, RFID technology remains the dominant mechanism for contactless identification, appearing in 81.8% of the reviewed literature. These systems have evolved to include "Dual-Punch" logic, utilizing GPS modules to record boarding and alighting coordinates to automate distance-based fare calculations. A critical innovation identified in the synthesis is the implementation of offline-first storage logic. This architecture ensures that transaction data is preserved in local memory during network "dead zones," which is vital for preventing revenue leakage and conductor malpractice in rugged transit environments.
Significant advancements have also been made in Automated Passenger Counting (APC) to eliminate the "Human Error Fallacy" associated with manual monitoring. While traditional systems relied on basic infrared or ultrasonic sensors, newer implementations utilize advanced machine learning techniques, such as the FOMO algorithm on ESP32-CAM modules, which can achieve high precision. Furthermore, dual-verification architectures that synchronize optical sensors with RFID data have demonstrated the potential to reach 100% counting accuracy. These automated mechanisms ensure that digital manifests match actual vehicle occupancy without human intervention.
Despite these technological strides, several research gaps remain that directly inform the development of the proposed system. The literature indicates a lack of unified, portable handheld devices that integrate high-precision passenger counting, as most advanced features are currently confined to fixed gate infrastructure. Additionally, there is a significant void regarding the use of reusable, seat-based RFID markers, which are more practical for high-turnover public utility vehicles than individual smart card ownership. Most existing transport research also prioritizes standardized metro systems, leaving a scarcity of low-cost, open-source solutions tailored for the unique, "konduktor-led" operations characteristic of the Philippine transport sector. This study aims to address these deficiencies by developing a localized, offline-ready platform specifically designed for the minibus environment in Iligan City.


2.4. Conclusion
This systematic literature review (SLR) analyzed 22 studies on microcontroller-based POS systems and passenger monitoring in public transportation. The findings showed a shift from Arduino and basic RFID systems to ESP32-based technologies with IoT capabilities, GPS integration, and advanced Automated Passenger Counting (APC) systems for more accurate and automated fare collection.
The review also identified common challenges such as unstable internet connectivity, expensive proprietary systems, and the “Human Error Fallacy” in manual passenger counting. A major research gap found was the lack of a unified and portable system that combines fare collection and automatic passenger monitoring, especially for “konduktor-led” public utility vehicles in cities like Iligan.
These findings served as the foundation for the proposed RFID-Based POS and Passenger Monitoring System. The study aims to develop a low-cost, open-source, and localized solution that improves revenue protection, passenger monitoring, and operational efficiency through automation
